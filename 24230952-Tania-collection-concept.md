I took Valorant as my subject to record. It is a first player shooting game mainly on pc. Characters (agents) have their unique abilities and the goal is to be the first to win 13 rounds by completing role-specific objectives or eliminating the opposing team. The way it is disappearing is related to the experience I had in game and also watching tournaments. It is still here but changing frequently. Also, it is easy to focus on the competitive rank, and letting those experiences in game fade away easily. Besides, it is also something that cannot go back to record it again if a new patch was released.
Why I took this subject is because the term "taking records" brought me back to what my friend told me before. He was excited, telling us about a game course he took, in which they are required to submit their battle records as an in-class task. I then think that I can also try to record my gaming experience for this homework. 
The twenty records can be quite different when it comes to actual gaming and attending its live esports events. As most of the records are from the game, this makes watching the tournament the odd one out. I still keep it because they all revolve around that specific game and show how it greatly impacted my life. I also wish I could remember the time that I traveled just to see that match. 
Other than that, from what was not captured, the underlying feelings experienced during those moments are something hard or cannot hold in common. Also for the adrenaline rush in games. It is more significant when it comes to a non-gamer viewing it, as they might feel so different from the collection. In addition, I think it will be better if I can replace most of the images with videos. However, they are something that I could not make as they were captured some time ago, back in the days when the game did not have gameplay captures built-in. That is why I was only able to take a screenshot to record.
